## Nearest partial precedents

### Exact and vector-potential flow constructions

- Baron (2025) derives exact incompressible unsteady Navier–Stokes solutions
  from special vector-valued stream functions and places known Beltrami flows
  in that class. It does not begin with an observed recurrent streamline or
  include peripheral sensing, cross-host certification, Lean, or Earth scaling.
  <https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0287737>
- Ohkitani (2015) develops vector-potential equations for smooth
  incompressible flows and discusses relevance to Navier–Stokes regularity. It
  does not provide the present realization chain.
  <https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.92.033010>

### Recurrence and sensing

- Lichtenegger et al. (2025) use data-assisted propagators around recurrent
  reference flows. Their recurrence is a model-reduction strategy, not a
  source-bound geometric carrier with observational zero and formal
  certification. <https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0306402>
- A 2025 Nature Communications study shows that sensing flow gradients is
  necessary for learned underwater navigation. Its sensors guide an agent in a
  driven environmental flow; they are not passive full-arclength whiskers on an
  unforced realized body. <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-58125-6>

### Rigorous computation and formalization

- Chernyshenko et al. (2007) establish an a posteriori route by which numerical
  Galerkin computations can certify a strong 3-D Navier–Stokes solution for a
  particular initial condition. This is the closest rigorous-computation
  precedent for a future universality gate, but it does not contain the present
  geometry, sensing, or Lean-bound chain.
  <https://doi.org/10.1063/1.2372512>
- Wang (2026) formalizes the Galerkin construction and energy inequality for
  unforced two-dimensional Navier–Stokes in Lean. It is an analytic existence
  formalization, not a formal certificate for an observed three-dimensional
  carrier realization. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33033>
- Formal reproducibility tenets have been applied to workflows using different
  FFT implementations, demonstrating how small cross-platform differences can
  be exposed and classified. That work does not bind a Navier–Stokes carrier to
  Lean or physical scaling. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.future.2024.107684>
